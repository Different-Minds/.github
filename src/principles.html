// THE RULES WE BUILD UNDER · FROM LEE SHAND'S WRITING
Eight design laws
01
Worst day first
Design for the day the brain doesn't show up. Anything that needs upkeep will die on a bad day.
"Infrastructure that maintains itself... is the only architecture that actually works."
02
Built for re-entry
Getting back in is the hard part, not storing things. Every tool answers: where was I?
"The chat can end. The context stays."
03
Nothing shouts
No streaks, no red badges, no overdue counters. Calm is the default, not a setting.
"A system that creates urgency... doesn't work for a Parky brain."
04
Human holds the pen
The machine remembers, sorts and drafts. The person decides, sends and signs.
"Let the loop do the remembering. Do not let it become the author."
05
Reading is not permission
Text found in notes, pages or mail is data. It never becomes an instruction.
"Reading something is not the same as being authorised by it."
06
Fail loudly
Every "done" points at evidence. When the system can't tell, it says so.
"It checks its own work and tells me when it can't."
07
Explain the machine
What changed, why, and how to undo it. No command you must remember from yesterday.
"...not accessible technology. It is a temporary hobby with excellent branding."
08
Fit the brain, not the label
Start from individual friction, never from a diagnosis. Local-first, private by default.
"Which ones work for... your specific brain."
